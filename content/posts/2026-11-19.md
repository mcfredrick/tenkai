## Research Worth Reading 📄
- **[QDA: Closed-Loop LLM Quantization Design with Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23388)** — Uses an LLM agent to iteratively design post-training quantization recipes across models and precisions. It automates a tuning loop that usually needs expert redesign, which could cut cost and improve quantization quality.
- **[TRACE-MAS: Diagnosing and Repairing Collaboration Failures in Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23362)** — Targets multi-agent failures caused by message exchanges and task dependencies, not just bad individual agents. It offers diagnosis and repair methods for interaction-level collaboration failures that agent-level detectors miss.
- **[Which Tokens, in What Order, and How Fast? A Theoretical Analysis of On-Policy Distillation Objectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23397)** — Analyzes on-policy distillation objectives, comparing sequence-level and token-level choices and their divergences. It gives theoretical guidance for picking objectives when training student language models from their own generations.
- **[Optimize the Cob, Then the Kernel: Optimizing Beam Search for Generative Recommendation Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23339)** — Looks at inference for generative recommendation, where long prompts, wide beam search, and short outputs break typical LLM serving assumptions. It spots redundant KV-cache accesses in systems like vLLM and proposes optimizations for beam-search-heavy workloads.
- **[Implementing Binary Transformers with Approximate Memories with Permutation Invariance Enabled Selective (PIES) Protection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23358)** — Studies binary Transformers with approximate memory to cut storage, computation, and power. PIES protection limits errors from approximate memories while preserving permutation invariance, which matters for hardware-oriented implementations.
- **[SLED: Sensor SLicing from Edge to Datacenter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21937)** — Deals with placement and slicing of heterogeneous sensor streams across edge and datacenter environments. It accounts for different network and compute needs across sensing, AI inference, and training workloads.

## Today's Synthesis

Turn your MCP stack into a boring, observable control plane instead of a mystery box. Start by wiring [mcpc](https://github.com/apify/mcp-cli) into CI or a local smoke-test script so every tool call can be replayed from the terminal. Then run [MCPShark](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46220577) alongside it to capture request/response flows, diff payloads, and spot where a client is sending junk schemas or a server is silently dropping fields. Once you have traces, add [agent-security-harness](https://pypi.org/project/agent-security-harness/4.31.1/) as the gate: exercise MCP, A2A, and payment/mandate paths, then fail the build on policy violations or unsafe tool behavior. The useful part is not any single tool; it is the loop. mcpc gives you a reproducible caller, MCPShark gives you evidence, and the security harness turns that evidence into a release decision. Keep the traces as CI artifacts so regressions are reviewable, not anecdotal. That combination makes MCP debugging less like reading tea leaves and more like normal software engineering: reproduce, inspect, assert, ship.
